# Source 01: Continuous Ultrafiltration for Congestive Heart Failure (The CUORE Trial)

- **Original URL**: https://onlinejcf.com/article/S1071-9164(13)01254-2/abstract
- **Citation**: Marenzi G, Muratori M, Cosentino ER, et al. Continuous Ultrafiltration for Congestive Heart Failure: The CUORE Trial. *Journal of Cardiac Failure*. 2014;20(1):9–17.
- **Study Design**: Prospective, randomized, controlled, open-label clinical trial (1:1 allocation).
- **Population**: Hospitalized patients with congestive heart failure and overt fluid overload.
- **Sample Size**: Total **56 patients** (First-line Ultrafiltration group: `n = 27`; Standard medical/diuretic therapy control group: `n = 29`).
- **Intervention vs. Control**: First-line continuous extracorporeal ultrafiltration vs. standard intravenous diuretic medical therapy.
- **Blinding**: Open-label (non-blinded) due to mechanical extracorporeal ultrafiltration device vs. drug administration.
- **Primary Endpoint**: Rehospitalizations for congestive heart failure during a 1-year follow-up period.
- **Key Findings**: Ultrafiltration was associated with a lower incidence of 1-year heart failure rehospitalization compared with standard diuretic therapy, despite similar initial weight loss.
- **Methodological Notes for RoB 2**:
  - Small sample size (`n = 56`), open-label design.
  - Primary outcome (CHF rehospitalization) involves clinical admission decisions that could potentially be influenced if clinicians know prior treatment assignment or follow-up care differs.
