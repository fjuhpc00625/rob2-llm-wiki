# Source 02: Extracorporeal Ultrafiltration vs. Conventional Diuretic Therapy in Advanced Decompensated Heart Failure

- **Original URL**: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-7133.2011.00231.x
- **Citation**: Hanna MA, Tang WHW, Teo BW, O'Neill JO, Weinstein DM, Lau SM, Van Lente F, Starling RC, Paganini EP, Taylor DO. Extracorporeal Ultrafiltration vs. Conventional Diuretic Therapy in Advanced Decompensated Heart Failure. *Congestive Heart Failure*. 2012;18(1):54–63. (Epub 2011 Jul 20). DOI: 10.1111/j.1751-7133.2011.00231.x.
- **Study Design**: Single-center, prospective, randomized, controlled, open-label trial under hemodynamically guided therapy in an ICU setting.
- **Population**: Patients admitted to an intensive care unit with advanced decompensated heart failure requiring hemodynamically guided therapy.
- **Sample Size**: Total **36 patients** (Extracorporeal Ultrafiltration [UF]: `n = 17`; Conventional Diuretic [CD] therapy: `n = 19`).
- **Intervention vs. Control**: Extracorporeal ultrafiltration vs. conventional intravenous diuretic therapy.
- **Blinding**: Open-label (patients and ICU clinicians unblinded because of mechanical filtration circuit vs. diuretic drugs).
- **Primary Endpoint**: Time to achieve and maintain pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≤ 18 mm Hg for ≥ 4 consecutive hours; secondary endpoints included weight reduction, total fluid volume removal, hospital length of stay, kidney function, biomarkers, and adverse events.
- **Key Findings**: UF group achieved greater weight reduction, higher total fluid removal, and shorter hospital length of stay, with no significant differences in adverse events, kidney function changes, or biomarker levels.
- **Methodological Notes for RoB 2**:
  - Very small single-center ICU trial (`n = 36`).
  - Open-label design, though hemodynamic target (PCWP ≤ 18 mm Hg) and lab biomarkers are objective physiological measurements.
