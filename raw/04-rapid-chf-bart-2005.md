# Source 04: Ultrafiltration Versus Usual Care for Hospitalized Patients with Heart Failure (The RAPID-CHF Trial)

- **Original URL**: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0735109705022060?via%3Dihub
- **Citation**: Bart BA, Boyle A, Bank AJ, Anand I, Olivari MT, Kraemer M, Mackedanz S, Sobotka PA, Schollmeyer M, Goldsmith SR. Ultrafiltration versus usual care for hospitalized patients with heart failure: the Relief for Acutely Fluid-Overloaded Patients With Decompensated Congestive Heart Failure (RAPID-CHF) trial. *Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC)*. 2005;46(11):2043–2046. DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2005.05.098.
- **Study Design**: Multicenter, prospective, randomized, controlled, open-label pilot feasibility trial (1:1 randomization).
- **Population**: Hospitalized patients with acute decompensated heart failure and evidence of volume overload.
- **Sample Size**: Total **40 patients** (Ultrafiltration + usual care: `n = 20`; Usual care alone: `n = 20`).
- **Intervention vs. Control**: Single 8-hour ultrafiltration session plus usual care vs. usual care alone.
- **Blinding**: Open-label (non-blinded due to extracorporeal ultrafiltration device).
- **Primary Endpoint**: Weight loss at 24 hours after enrollment.
- **Key Findings**: Weight loss at 24 hours was 2.5 kg (UF) vs. 1.86 kg (usual care), not statistically significant (`p = 0.240`), although net fluid removal at 24 hours was significantly higher in the UF group.
- **Methodological Notes for RoB 2**:
  - Pilot feasibility study (`n = 40`), open-label.
  - Primary outcome (**weight loss at 24 hours**) is an **objective measurement** (scale reading), making D4 (measurement of outcome) a classic teaching example where open-label does NOT automatically mean High risk!
  - Published in 2005 (pre-mandatory detailed SAP era), so pre-specified analysis plan details are limited.
